December 7, 1862. Brigadier General James G. Blunt's troops approached Prairie Grove from the northwest. To the east, General Herron's men were already heavily engaged with Confederate forces holding positions along the ridge.

Communication was imperfect, information was incomplete, and commanders often had only minutes to make decisions. Success depended upon timing, coordination, and the ability to adapt to changing battlefield conditions.

As commander of Blunt's force, consider the choices that confronted Union officers throughout the day. How would your decisions shape the battle's outcome?

Historical Note: This activity is based on documented actions and battlefield conditions associated with Brigadier General James G. Blunt's command during the Battle of Prairie Grove. Outcomes have been simplified for educational interpretation and to illustrate the challenges of Civil War battlefield leadership.
